The Importance of Dental Care
Teeth are no longer about [treating pain after it occurs]- it's now about [preventing it in advance]that matters most.
As lifespans grow longer, keeping teeth healthy for a long time so you can chew well becomes increasingly important.
BOA Dental Clinic is a tooth-preserving clinic that provides differentiated care tailored to each life stage.
Care by Life Stage
Dental Care for Pregnant Women
  • The more cavity-causing bacteria a mother has, the more such bacteria the baby is likely to acquire, increasing the child's risk of cavities.
  • If a pregnant woman develops periodontal disease, the risk of "preterm birth or low birth weight" is said to increase.
  • It is advisable to receive oral care guidance from a dental clinic starting in early pregnancy.
Infant Dental Care (Ages 0-4)
  • Helping the child get used to a toothbrush and remember brushingas something enjoyable.
  • Baby teeth often have gaps between them where cavities can easily form, so careful observation is needed.
Dental Care for Children & Adolescents
  • Children who breathe through their mouth tend to have a dry oral environment, making them more prone to cavities and malocclusionaccording to reports.
  • In some cases, oral myofunctional therapy (MFT) or orthodontic treatment may be needed.
Dental Care for Adults
  • Those who notice symptoms such as multiple cavities, swollen or bleeding gums, bad breath, or tooth loss from untreated issues should take extra care.
  • At this age, regular dental check-ups, scaling, and improved oral care habits can help prevent future diseasefrom developing.
Elderly Age Group
  • There is a close relationship between oral health and systemic disease- for example, periodontal disease is linked to diabetes, and periodontal disease is linked to cerebrovascular disorders.
  • Recently, it has been noted that the fewer remaining teeth a person has, the higher their risk of developing dementia.
